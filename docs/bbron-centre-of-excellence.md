# Make the Centre the Brand

_A discussion paper on how OrthoPilot Elite competes in a market that has stopped buying robots and started buying patient pull._

_Prepared for Matt Graham, B. Braun UK, by AGENCY Bristol. This is thinking material for internal discussion, not a costed proposal._

---

## Background — the problem

The UK knee-arthroplasty market has been shaped by a story that equates "robot" with "modern surgery" and "best outcome." Stryker has now moved the battleground further forward: its national direct-to-patient campaign ("Scan. Plan. Mako Can.") is actively telling patients which sites to ask for.

The published literature is more qualified than the marketing. Radiographic alignment is reliably improved by robot-assisted systems; patient-reported outcomes and long-term survivorship are contested. Independent reviews — JAAOS 2024 on surgeon marketing claims, the American Joint Replacement Registry on 5-year survivorship — have flagged the gap between the story and the evidence.

OrthoPilot Elite has structural advantages within that gap: image-free (no CT-planning lead-time), a fraction of the capital cost, faster set-up. Its case on alignment is well-evidenced. Its case on functional outcomes versus current-generation robots is contested, not conceded.

### The implication

**The commercial race in this market is no longer primarily about the device. It is about where patients go when they decide they want a knee replacement.** Stryker saw this first and is spending accordingly. A defence of OrthoPilot that stays at the device level concedes that race.

The question for B. Braun is whether to play a different one.

---

## Vision of success

B. Braun partners with a UK host — private group or NHS trust — to build a **knee Centre of Excellence**: a destination clinic that becomes a brand in its own right. The centre is what patients, GPs and insurers hear about. OrthoPilot Elite is the engine inside it, not the story.

What success could look like twelve months in:

- **The centre is the thing patients search for and ask to be referred to.** Stryker tells patients to look for Mako; we'd tell them to look for the centre by name. Brand pull sits at the destination, not the device.
- **A measured patient pipeline flows into the centre.** Self-referrals and GP referrals tracked as the primary KPI, together with appointment-to-surgery conversion and list-fill rate. Insurer pathways evaluated as a second-stage extension once the network case is made.
- **The host hospital gains list volume.** The commercial leverage moves from kit to funnel: the host's growth comes from patients arriving, not from owning capital.
- **OrthoPilot Elite carries the operational volume.** Image-free workflow, no CT-planning lead-time, and faster set-up are why the centre can run the throughput the marketing brings in. The throughput case is well-supported in principle and we'd want UK time-and-motion data from a live OrthoPilot site to anchor the projection before any commercial commitment.
- **The centre becomes a credible reference site.** Case volume, patient experience data, and commercial outcomes from one place underwrite the next sell.

There is a tension worth surfacing: a single-host funnel concentrates volume at one site and reduces flow to other UK OrthoPilot accounts. Whether that's a feature (reference site, depth) or a bug (channel cannibalisation) depends on the wider B. Braun UK commercial picture — Matt is closer to that than we are.

## Strands of the strategy — what's worth working through

The work that sits behind the vision splits into four strands. Treat these as the conversation, not a deliverable list.

1. **Go-to-market design for the centre.** Positioning, naming, brand frame, audience segmentation, and the editorial line a hospital is comfortable carrying.
2. **Patient-acquisition.** Content, targeted digital, PR, and the referral-network work with GPs that turns awareness into a pipeline. The bigger end of this is the strand where comparable destination-clinic precedents exist worth studying — Cromwell Knee Unit, Schoen Clinic London, Fortius — at varying scales of marketing investment.
3. **Referral plumbing.** The practical infrastructure that turns "I've heard of the centre" into a booked consultation. Sits between marketing and the host hospital's own systems; scope depends on what the host already has.
4. **Measurement.** A shared view between B. Braun, the host, and AGENCY of what good looks like — pipeline volume, conversion, list utilisation, patient experience.

Lighter and heavier versions of each strand exist. Sales-enablement-only is a sensible light version; a fully run programme with AGENCY operating the acquisition engine is the heavy version. The right shape depends on B. Braun's appetite and budget envelope.

## What we'd want from B. Braun's side to take this forward

Five questions whose answers determine the shape of any next step:

1. **Direction.** Sales-enablement story for B. Braun's existing UK reps to carry into private groups, or a centre-and-funnel partnership of the kind described above? They're different sizes of engagement.
2. **Host.** Is there a realistic first host — a private group or trust already close enough to B. Braun that this conversation is plausible in the next quarter or two?
3. **Compliance pre-condition.** B. Braun-funded patient marketing routed through a hospital brand sits inside the ABHI Code of Practice and MHRA Blue Guide on advertising medical devices to the public. We'd want a written compliance read from B. Braun before any patient-facing creative is designed — not after. Flagging this as a real constraint, not a footnote.
4. **Budget envelope.** Indicative budget orientation from B. Braun's side — what scale of programme is plausible internally, drawing on relevant reference points (IPL documents, comparable activity). We'd cost the strategy after this, not before.
5. **AGENCY's role.** Where we own delivery, where we design only, where the host or B. Braun in-house carries it.
