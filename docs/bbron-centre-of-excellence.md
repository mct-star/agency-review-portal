# Make the Centre the Brand

_A destination-led play for OrthoPilot Elite. Draft for Matt Graham, B. Braun._
_Prepared by AGENCY Bristol._

---

## Background — the problem

The UK knee-arthroplasty market has been shaped by a story that equates "robot" with "modern surgery" and "best outcome." Private groups have followed that story, paying around £1M of capital plus ongoing implant lock-in, because it helps them attract patients and retain consultant referrers. Stryker has now moved the battleground further forward: its national direct-to-patient campaign ("Scan. Plan. Mako Can.") is actively telling patients to go to Mako-equipped sites.

The published literature is more qualified than the marketing. Radiographic alignment is reliably improved by robot-assisted systems; patient-reported outcomes and long-term survivorship are contested. Independent reviews (including JAAOS 2024 on surgeon marketing claims, and the American Joint Replacement Registry work on 5-year survivorship) have flagged the gap between the story and the evidence.

OrthoPilot Elite sits outside the capital-robot narrative in ways that matter commercially: image-free, a fraction of the capital cost, faster set-up, no CT-planning lead-time. Its clinical case on alignment is well-evidenced. Its case on functional outcomes versus current-generation robots is contested, not conceded.

### The implication

**The commercial race in this market is no longer primarily about the device. It is about where patients go when they decide they want a knee replacement.** Stryker saw this first and is spending accordingly. Hospitals need patients more than they need another capital robot. A defence of OrthoPilot that stays at the device level concedes that race.

The question for B. Braun is whether to play a different one.

---

## Vision of success

B. Braun partners with a named UK host — private group or NHS trust — to build a **knee Centre of Excellence**: a destination clinic that becomes a brand in its own right. The centre is what patients, GPs and insurers hear about. OrthoPilot Elite is the engine inside it, not the story.

What success looks like twelve months in:

- **The centre is the thing patients search for and ask to be referred to.** Stryker tells patients to look for Mako; we tell them to look for [the Centre]. Brand pull sits at the destination, not the device.
- **A measured patient pipeline flows into the centre.** Self-referrals, GP referrals, and insurer-directed referrals tracked as the primary KPI, together with appointment-to-surgery conversion and list-fill rate.
- **The host hospital gains list volume and margin** — not a £1M capex line. The commercial leverage is the funnel, not the kit.
- **OrthoPilot Elite delivers the volume** the marketing brings in. Faster theatre turnaround and no CT-planning lead-time are the reasons the centre can physically run the throughput without adding staff.
- **The centre becomes B. Braun's reference site.** Case volume, patient experience data and commercial returns all come from one place, and underwrite the next sell.

## What the engagement would involve

To make this honest, the shape of what we'd be proposing to deliver is:

1. **Go-to-market design** for the centre — positioning, naming, brand frame, segmentation.
2. **Patient-acquisition engine** — content, targeted digital, PR, and referral-network work with GPs and insurers. Measured on pipeline volume and conversion, not on creative output.
3. **Referral and signposting infrastructure** — the practical plumbing that turns "I've heard of the centre" into a booked consultation.
4. **Measurement framework** — a shared dashboard between B. Braun, the host hospital, and us.

This is a partnership engagement, not a messaging refresh, and we'd scope and price it as one.

---

## What we'd want to agree with B. Braun before going further

Three things, in order:

1. **The host.** Which private group or trust is the right first partner, and at what commitment level.
2. **Clinical and evidence boundaries.** What claims B. Braun is willing to stand behind in patient-facing content, and where MHRA/ABHI guardrails sit.
3. **The role of AGENCY Bristol in the build.** Where we own delivery, where we design only, and what the first twelve months look like commercially.
